Instructor-Led Training

Veterans Affairs Canada Project

A complete instructor-led training package that helps veterans apply strength and conditioning, nutrition and mindset strategies to their lives after service, built in partnership with subject matter experts.

  • ChallengeSMEs had deep expertise but needed it turned into structured, engaging instructor-led learning that veterans could apply in civilian life.
  • Approach Creative and engaging powerpoint slides, vyond videos, and a Canva participant workbook pairing hands-on activities with reflection prompts.
  • Result A structured and practical course with interactive sessions where learners practiced as they learned with guidance.

Working with subject matter experts

The project began with Veterans Affairs Canada subject matter experts. Before designing anything, I worked with them to understand who the learners were, what they already knew and where the knowledge gaps were, so the training would focus on what veterans actually needed.

  • Assessing knowledge gaps. Conversations with the SMEs clarified what learners already knew about training, nutrition and mindset, and where they needed the most support.
  • Agreeing on the approach. Together we decided how the content would be organized, which formats would serve the learners best, and how the instructor-led sessions would work.
  • Turning expertise into learning. I translated specialist content into clear learning outcomes and practical activities, and the SMEs reviewed the materials to confirm accuracy.

Intended learning outcomes

1. Understand and apply the Gary Roberts Performance methodology to strength, conditioning and mobility.

  • 1.1Use the TrainHeroic app to log and track your strength and conditioning sessions.
  • 1.2Scale your training to a home or commercial gym with minimal equipment as necessary.
  • 1.3Perform various exercises correctly and safely.

2. Build your nutrition foundation and deep dive into periodized and goal-oriented nutritional planning.

  • 2.1Analyze your current food intake and apply basic nutritional principles to enhance overall health and longevity.
  • 2.2Understand how to fuel for performance and optimize your carbohydrate intake around training days.
  • 2.3Examine ways to optimize your nutrition for your goals, such as strength and hypertrophy and body composition.
  • 2.4Identify adaptations to your nutrition strategies to account for your current stressors, lifestyle and schedule.

3. Recognize a growth mindset and apply the EAF Framework.

  • 3.1Apply the EAF (effort, attitude and opportunity) framework to your life after service.
  • 3.2Build on practical tools such as goal-setting, habit stacking and building a high-performance identity in civilian life.

Resources I created

  • Animated video (Vyond). A short animated video that introduces key ideas visually and gives learners a clear, engaging way into the content.
  • PowerPoint slides for instructors. A slide deck that guides the instructor through each session, so delivery is consistent and keeps to the learning outcomes.
  • Participant workbook. A workbook that follows along with the slides, with hands-on activities and reflection prompts so learners practice and apply ideas during the session.

How I organized the content and built learner engagement

  • Chunking. I divided the content into three focused topics (training, nutrition and mindset) and broke each into smaller segments tied to a single outcome, so learners could take in one idea at a time and the sessions stayed manageable.
  • Dual coding. The video, slides and workbook pair visuals with concise text, so learners can connect each idea to an image as well as words, which supports understanding and recall.
  • Aligned resources. The slides and workbook follow the same order, so learners always know where they are and the materials reinforce each other.
  • Active practice and reflection. Activities and prompts ask learners to apply each idea to their own training, nutrition and life after service, which makes the content personally relevant.

Learning theory and design process

  • ADDIE. I used ADDIE to give the project a clear structure: analyzing the learners and gaps with the SMEs, designing outcomes and activities, developing the video, slides and workbook, delivering through instructor-led sessions, and evaluating what worked.
  • SAM (Successive Approximation Model). I built and shared early drafts for review, then refined them across several rounds of feedback from the SMEs, so the materials improved step by step instead of waiting for one final review.
  • Social learning theory. The instructor-led format lets learners learn from the facilitator and from each other through discussion, shared experience and peer examples, which is especially valuable for a group of veterans moving into civilian life.
